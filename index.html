How the chain unfolds

Six steps from tighter credit to a cleared chain, with the fork between the two scenarios. Show the logicHide

The bust starts in credit, not in usage. The small, levered buyers fail first, the financing chain around OpenAI breaks next, and the size of the damage then depends on one question: whether the capacity already installed becomes excess.

  1. 1

    Money gets dearer

    The Fed hiked in September for the first time since 2023, the 10-year Treasury yield is above 5%, and markets price more hikes into 2027. There is no rate-cut cushion of the kind that stopped the 2001 and 2008 financing shocks from becoming demand shocks.

    Effect on chipsNone yet. Orders still reflect last year's plans.
  2. 2

    The marginal buyers lose funding

    AI startups spending venture money on compute, roughly $65–105B a year by our estimate, cut back. Model-training startups with no revenue stop almost entirely; app-layer startups lose only the part their losses were funding. Directly, this is only 5–15% of the loss on this page, but these startups are the neoclouds' customers.

    Effect on chipsNeocloud utilisation and renewal prices weaken.
  3. 3

    Neoclouds can't refinance

    Neoclouds borrow for about five years against customer contracts averaging about three, so lenders are underwriting renewals. Once default swap spreads pass 1,000 basis points, the market treats the credit as distressed and refinancing shuts.

    Effect on chipsThe fastest-growing slice of Nvidia's customers stops ordering.
  4. 4

    The financing chain breaks

    OpenAI relies on capital raised against future revenue. SoftBank funded its stake with bridge and margin loans. Oracle signed $288B of data-centre leases, largely for OpenAI's sites. When one link fails the others follow. Oracle survives by walking away from new builds, which passes the loss to developers and their lenders.

    Effect on chipsOpenAI's programmes with Broadcom, AMD and Nvidia are lost, along with the capacity contracted for it.
  5. 5

    The fork: is installed capacity scarce or in excess?

    This decides whether the shock stays in finance or reaches demand. The trigger matters less than whether the chips already running become surplus. Telecom in 2001 was financing-led too, but fibre had been overbuilt many times over.

Where the losses come from

    What would tip financing-led into full bust

    1. GPU rental prices falling, especially one-year contracts.
    2. Labs cutting token prices without faster revenue growth.
    3. Private-credit funds limiting withdrawals, or GPU-backed loans trading below about 80 cents.
    4. Memory contract prices rolling over.
    5. Hyperscalers describing capex as digestion, or writing down GPUs.
    6. Unemployment rising and enterprise software guidance cut.

    The first two decide it. While rental prices and revenue per token hold, a financing shock stays a financing shock. Track both sets of signals in the watch list.

    How to read this

    The two scenarios

    • Full bust: the levered buyers fail and usage disappoints too. Foreclosed capacity floods the rental market, and hyperscalers cut.
    • Financing-led: credit shuts for the levered buyers while usage keeps growing. New builds stop, installed capacity stays scarce, and cash-rich buyers take the sites.
    • In both, OpenAI's forward orders are lost. The pressure points fall in the first half of 2027: SoftBank's bridge loan, OpenAI's delayed listing and Oracle's funding plan.

    What each bar means

    • Today is the 2026 estimate, built from reported results for the first three quarters.
    • Destroyed or delayed is the 2028 base case minus the trough year. The base is higher than today, so the loss can exceed today's pool.
    • Destroyed is price, margin and orders that do not return. Delayed is spending pushed into 2030 to 2032.
    • Profit pools add up across layers. Revenue does not.

    Limits and anchors

    • The 2028 base rolls forward vendor guidance, which already sums to more than buyers' announced capex can absorb.
    • Rows marked rough have no reported anchor. Intel Foundry revenue is mostly internal.
    • Today's figures: Samsung, SK hynix, Micron, Nvidia, Broadcom, AMD, Intel and TSMC third-quarter results; TrendForce, WSTS and SEMI forecasts.
    • Chain of events: Schwab on the September hike and 5% yields; Oracle's 10-Q lease note via this summary; CoreWeave's August loan terms via The Energy Mag. The startup pool is our estimate from Crunchbase, Dealroom and reported run rates.
    • Spreads: press reports of ICE, Markit, LSEG and S&P Global data, including Reuters, CNBC and Bloomberg. Vendors differ by a few points.
